What is "vibe coding" anyway? A plain-English guide

G
Gavin Magid
12 August 2026 · 6 min read

I'll admit something: the first time a client asked me "so is this whole vibe coding thing actually a real thing, or is it just a TikTok trend," I laughed, because that's a fair question. It sounds made up. It kind of is made up, in the way most tech terms are made up. But what it points at is real, and if you run a business, it's worth five minutes of your time.

Here's the short version: vibe coding means building software by describing what you want in plain language, instead of typing out every line of code by hand. You say "I need a page where clients can book a session and get a confirmation email," and the AI generates working code for that. A person (me, in this case) still has to check it, shape it, test it, and make sure it actually fits how your business works. The slow, expensive part is writing thousands of lines of code from scratch, and that now happens in minutes instead of weeks.

Why this actually matters to you

Before this, if you wanted something custom built (a booking system, a client portal, an internal tool to replace your spreadsheet chaos) you had two options. Pay a software developer a small fortune and wait months. Or don't bother, and keep limping along with WhatsApp, Excel, and hope.

Most self-employed professionals I talk to chose door number two. Not because they didn't want better systems, but because the cost and time never made sense for a one-person or five-person practice.

That math has changed. Not because AI is magic, but because it removes the slowest, most expensive part of building software: the typing. What's left is the part that actually needs a human, understanding your business, making good design decisions, and testing that the thing works before it touches a real client.

It's not that AI replaced the developer. It's that the developer stopped spending 80% of their time on typing, and started spending it on judgement.

What it doesn't mean

It doesn't mean you type a sentence and a perfect app appears, no human involved. Anyone who's actually used these tools knows that's not how it goes. It takes someone who knows what they're doing to steer it, catch the mistakes, and make it genuinely fit your business, the same as it always has. The difference is speed and cost, not the need for a professional.

It also doesn't mean "cheap and nasty." A site or tool built this way can be every bit as solid, secure and well-designed as one built the traditional way. I still design it properly. I still test it before you see it, and again before it goes live. The AI is a faster set of hands, not a replacement for the thinking.

What I'd actually use it for

In practice, for the clients I work with, this usually looks like: a booking or intake system that replaces a WhatsApp back-and-forth, a simple client dashboard that pulls everything into one place instead of three apps, or a website that would've cost R80,000 and taken two months, done properly in a couple of weeks for far less.

It's not the right fit for everything. If you need something extremely complex, heavily regulated, or safety-critical, that's a longer, more careful conversation. But for most of what a small practice or business actually needs, it's the difference between "we should really get around to that one day" and "that's live, and it's saving you three hours a week."

If you've got a system you've been meaning to fix, or a website that's embarrassing to send people to, that's exactly the conversation worth having.
